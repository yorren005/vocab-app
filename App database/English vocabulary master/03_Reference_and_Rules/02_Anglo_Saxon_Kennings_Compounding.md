# 🏹 Anglo-Saxon Kennings & Compounding Engine

A **kenning** is a circumlocutory, metaphorical compound expression used in Old English and Old Norse poetry in place of a familiar noun. Kennings ground abstract or mundane things in vivid, physical, tactile imagery.

### Classic Historical Kennings & Modern Equivalents

| Kenning (Literal Compound) | Target Referent | Poetic & Tactile Resonance |
|---|---|---|
| **Whale-road** (*hron-rād*) | The Ocean | Evokes the vast, untamed, cold marine highways traversed by sea leviathans. |
| **Sky-candle** (*heofon-candel*) | The Sun | Depicts the sun as a sacred, radiant beacon illuminating the earthly world. |
| **Bone-house** (*bān-hūs*) | The Human Body | Portrays the flesh as a mortal dwelling or fortress sheltering the skeletal frame. |
| **Mind-hoard** (*mōd-sefa*) | Memory / Consciousness | Frames thoughts and memories as treasured wealth guarded inside the mind. |
| **Battle-sweat** (*heaðo-swāt*) | Blood | Highlights the excruciating physical exertion and trauma of close-quarters combat. |
| **Wave-steed** (*ȳð-mearh*) | Longship / Galley | Compares the rhythmic bounding of a ship across swells to riding a warhorse. |
| **Raven-harvest** (*hræfn-wæstm*) | Slain Enemy Host | Visceral depiction of the dead left upon the field for scavenging ravens. |
