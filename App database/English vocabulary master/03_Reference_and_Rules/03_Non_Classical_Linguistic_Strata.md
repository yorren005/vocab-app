# 🏛️ The 8 Non-Classical Linguistic Strata

The English vocabulary outside of Latin and Greek derives from eight rich linguistic traditions:

1. **Old English (Anglo-Saxon)**: The visceral core of English (*gleed, sear, sunder, cleave, quell, harry, gloaming, swart, yearn, rue, churlish, clough, sward*).
2. **Old Norse (Danelaw)**: Hard consonants, rugged topography, and martial kinetics (*scree, scathe, murk, bleak, skulk, ransack, berserk, mire, scowl, gawk*).
3. **Celtic & Gaelic**: Highlands topography and ancient wilderness (*crag, tor, cairn, glen, bog, slogan, bard*).
4. **Germanic & Yiddish**: Complex existential and psychological states (*weltschmerz, angst, schadenfreude, chutzpah, mensch, kvetch*).
5. **Persian & Arabic**: Eastern science, luxury, and celestial terms (*zenith, nadir, cipher, elixir, talisman, azure, bazaar, caravan*).
6. **Sanskrit & Hindi**: Cosmic forces, spiritual dynamics, and wilderness (*juggernaut, avatar, nirvana, karma, pundit, guru, jungle*).
7. **Japanese Aesthetic Lexis**: Meditative nature and transience (*wabi-sabi, kintsugi, komorebi, bokeh*).
8. **Slavic & Global Wilderness**: Vast terrains and extreme climates (*taiga, steppe, mammoth, tundra, squall*).
