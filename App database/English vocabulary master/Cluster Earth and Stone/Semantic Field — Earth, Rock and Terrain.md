# ⛰️ SEMANTIC FIELD: EARTH, ROCK AND TERRAIN

> [!info] 🏷️ Semantic Field Architecture
> **Core Concept:** Granite crags, sliding scree, ancient barrows, deep cloughs, and stony strata.
> **Linguistic Strata:** Exclusively non-Greek, non-Latin (Old English, Old Norse, Celtic, Germanic, and historical non-classical loanwords).
> **Data Grounding:** Senses, glosses, and distinctions verified via **Lexicon MCP** (WordNet & Wiktionary engines).
> **Organizing Principle:** One concept → multidimensional network organized by intensity, mechanics, anatomy, sensory resonance, consequences, and metaphor.

---

## 1. Core Phenomenon
* **[[earth]]** — the ground, land
* **[[stone]]** — a lump or mass of hard consolidated mineral matter
* **[[rock]]** — a lump or mass of hard consolidated mineral matter
* **[[ground]]** — the solid part of the earth's surface
* **[[soil]]** — material in the top layer of the surface of the earth in which plants can grow (especially with reference to its quality or use)

---

## 2. Initiation & Formation (Sedimentation & Heaping)
* **[[silt]]** — fine earth deposited by water
* **[[settle]]** — sink down or precipitate
* **[[layer]]** — thin structure composed of a single thickness of cells
* **[[heap]]** — pile in a heap
* **[[crust]]** — a hard outer layer that covers something

---

## 3. Low Intensity / Fine Grains & Loose Earth
* **[[dust]]** — fine powdery material such as dry earth or pollen that can be blown about in the air
* **[[sand]]** — a loose material consisting of grains of rock or coral
* **[[grit]]** — a hard coarse-grained siliceous sandstone
* **[[gravel]]** — small fragments of rock
* **[[loam]]** — a rich soil consisting of a mixture of sand and clay and decaying organic materials
* **[[peat]]** — partially carbonized vegetable matter saturated with water; can be used as a fuel when dried

---

## 4. Increasing Intensity (Gradation Scale)
A progressive scalar escalation of intensity and manifestation:

> [!tip] 📈 Scalar Gradation Chain
> **[[dust]]** *(fine dry powdery mineral earth)*  
> ↳ **[[sand]]** *(loose granular mineral particles)*  
> ↳ **[[silt]]** *(fine sedimentary earth carried by water)*  
> ↳ **[[gravel]]** *(loose assemblage of small rounded stones)*  
> ↳ **[[pebble]]** *(small smooth stone worn by water)*  
> ↳ **[[stone]]** *(compact solid piece of mineral rock)*  
> ↳ **[[boulder]]** *(massive detached rounded rock)*  
> ↳ **[[tor]]** *(high exposed granite hill or rock tower)*  
> ↳ **[[crag]]** *(steep rugged precipitous rock face)*  
> ↳ **[[bedrock]]** *(solid unyielding subterranean foundation rock)*  

---

## 5. Imposing Formations & High Outcrops
* **[[crag]]** — a steep rugged rock or cliff
* **[[tor]]** — a high rocky hill
* **[[boulder]]** — a large smooth mass of rock detached from its place of origin
* **[[cliff]]** — a steep high face of rock
* **[[ridge]]** — a long narrow natural elevation or striation
* **[[barrow]]** — mound of earth and stones raised over a grave

---

## 6. Depressions, Gorges & Rifts
* **[[clough]]** — A narrow valley; a cleft in a hillside; a ravine, glen, or gorge
* **[[chine]]** — A steep-sided ravine leading from the top of a cliff down to the sea
* **[[ravine]]** — A deep narrow valley or gorge in the earth's surface worn by running water
* **[[dale]]** — an open river valley (in a hilly area)
* **[[dell]]** — A small, deep, and wooded valley or sunken area of ground, especially in the form of a natural hollow
* **[[pit]]** — archaeology: excavation hole

---

## 7. Sensory & Textural Dimensions
* **[[gritty]]** — containing sand or grit
* **[[rugged]]** — Broken into sharp or irregular points; uneven; not smooth; rough
* **[[stony]]** — abounding in rocks or stones
* **[[flinty]]** — Resembling or containing flint; hard like flint
* **[[stark]]** — to the fullest degree

---

## 8. Causes & Geological Triggers
* **[[scree]]** — loose stony debris on a slope
* **[[weathering]]** — The action of weather on objects exposed to it
* **[[slide]]** — (music) rapid sliding up or down the musical scale
* **[[shift]]** — (geology) a crack in the earth's crust resulting from the displacement of one side with respect to the other
* **[[heave]]** — an upward movement (especially a rhythmical rising and falling)

---

## 9. Consequences & Aftermath
* **[[scree]]** — loose stony debris on a slope
* **[[rubble]]** — the remains of something that has been destroyed or broken up
* **[[clod]]** — lump of something, especially of earth or clay
* **[[mound]]** — A natural elevation appearing as if thrown up artificially; a regular and isolated hill, hillock, or knoll
* **[[quarry]]** — a surface excavation for extracting stone or slate

---

## 10. Resistance & Bedrock Firmness
* **[[bedrock]]** — solid unweathered rock lying beneath surface deposits of soil
* **[[steadfast]]** — firm and dependable especially in loyalty
* **[[adamant]]** — An unspecified mineral or rock of virtually impenetrable hardness.; A substance that neutralizes lodestones
* **[[anchor]]** — secure a vessel with an anchor

---

## 11. Metaphorical & Psychological Extensions
* **[[grounded]]** — not allowed to fly
* **[[stony]]** — hard as granite
* **[[bedrock]]** — principles from which other truths can be derived
* **[[earthy]]** — hearty and lusty
