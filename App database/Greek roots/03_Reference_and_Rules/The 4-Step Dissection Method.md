# The 4-Step Dissection Method

> The standardized protocol for analyzing and deconstructing any advanced Greek-derived English word.

## The Dissection Protocol

| Step | Action | Diagnostic Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Step 1: Sever the Suffix** | Identify the trailing grammatical or disciplinary marker (`-logy`, `-itis`, `-oma`, `-ic`, `-ist`). | *What part of speech or academic/medical category does this term belong to?* |
| **Step 2: Isolate the Prefix** | Detect leading directional, quantitative, or negative morphemes (`a-`, `anti-`, `hyper-`, `hypo-`, `syn-`). | *What direction, orientation, or negation is acting upon the core?* |
| **Step 3: Extract the Primary Root(s)** | Identify the Greek lexical base (`cardi-`, `psych-`, `morph-`, `chron-`). | *What is the fundamental Greek thought-primitive or physical object?* |
| **Step 4: Synthesize the Semantic Rebuild** | Fuse the literal elements and translate into modern contextual English. | *What does the word mean literally vs. in modern technical application?* |

---

## Worked Dissection Examples

### Example 1: `Cardiomegaly`
- **Step 1 (Suffix):** `-y` (state or condition of).
- **Step 2 (Prefix):** None.
- **Step 3 (Roots):** `cardio-` (*kardía*, heart) + `megal-` (*mégas*, large/enlarged).
- **Step 4 (Rebuild):** *heart + enlarged + condition* → **Abnormal enlargement of the heart.**

### Example 2: `Hypercholesterolemia`
- **Step 1 (Suffix):** `-emia` (*haîma*, blood condition).
- **Step 2 (Prefix):** `hyper-` (*hypér*, excessive/above).
- **Step 3 (Roots):** `chol-` (bile) + `stere-` (solid) + `ol` (chemical alcohol).
- **Step 4 (Rebuild):** *excessive + solid-bile-alcohol + in blood* → **Excessive presence of cholesterol in the bloodstream.**
