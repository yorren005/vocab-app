## Case Study 1: `Transubstantiation` (Theological & Philosophical)

| Level | Morpheme Element | Linguistic Function & Etymology |
|---|---|---|
| **Prefix** | `trans-` | *Across, beyond, changing across boundaries* |
| **Sub-Prefix** | `sub-` | *Underneath, supporting base* |
| **Root** | `sta-` (*stāre*) | *To stand, to physically be established* |
| **Connective** | `-i-` | *Latin connective vowel* |
| **Suffix** | `-ation` (*-atio*) | *Abstract noun denoting the complete theological or metaphysical process* |
| **Synthesis** | **trans- + sub- + sta + -i- + -ation** | Literal: "Changing across the underlying substance that stands underneath." |

## Case Study 2: `Incontestable` (Legal & Juridical)

| Level | Morpheme Element | Linguistic Function & Etymology |
|---|---|---|
| **Prefix 1** | `in-` | *Negative prefix: not, without* |
| **Prefix 2** | `con-` (*com-*) | *Together, thoroughly* |
| **Root** | `test` (*testis*) | *Witness; to call to witness, solemnly affirm* |
| **Suffix** | `-able` (*-abilis*) | *Adjectival capability: capable of being* |
| **Synthesis** | **in- + con- + test + -able** | Literal: "Not capable of being witnessed against in court; indisputable." |
