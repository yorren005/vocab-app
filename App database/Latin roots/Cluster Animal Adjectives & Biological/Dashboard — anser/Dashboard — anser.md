## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 Morphological Mechanics & Derivational Patterns
> Because *ānser* was an elemental noun rather than a verbal root, it does not combine with Latin directional prefixes (*ad-, con-, dis-, ex-*). Instead, its English lexicon is generated entirely through **taxonomic Latinization**, **adjectival suffixation**, and **compound medical nomenclature**:
> 1. **Adjectival Suffixation (`-ine` / `-ous`):** 
>    - `anser-` + `-ine` (Latin *-īnus*, "belonging to, resembling") $\to$ [[anserine]] (both literal avian characteristic and figurative folly).
>    - `anser-` + `-ous` (Latin *-ōsus*, "full of") $\to$ [[anserous]] (silly, goose-like).
> 2. **Linnaean Systematics:** The bare nominative *Anser* serves as the genus designation for the true grey geese, while the ordinal suffix *-iformes* creates the waterfowl order [[Anseriformes]].
> 3. **Biomedical & Anatomical Compounds:** Preserved in classical anatomical Latin phrases (*pes anserinus*, *cutis anserina*) and isolated into biochemistry as the specialized muscular dipeptide [[anserine]].
