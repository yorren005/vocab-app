## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 How Derived Words are Formed
> The root **juven** operates across English through four distinct morphological stems:
> - **Adjectival Base:** *iuvenis* (stem `juven-`), source of *juvenile*, *juvenility*.
> - **Inchoative Verb Stem:** *iuvenēscere* ("to grow young", present participle *iuvenēscēns* → *juvenescence*, *juvenescent*).
> - **Contracted Comparative:** *iūnior* (from *\*iuweni̯ōs* → *junior*, *juniority*).
> - **Prefixal Renewal:** English *re-* ("again") + Latin *iuvenis* + verbal *-ate* → *rejuvenate*, *rejuvenation*, *rejuvenator*.
> - **Neuter Plural Noun:** Latin *iuvenīlia* (early creative works → *juvenilia*).
>
> By attaching Latin inchoative suffixes (*-esce*, *-escent*), comparative morphemes (*-ior*), and verbalizers (*-ate*), English captures youth as an adjective, a comparative rank, a developmental process, and an act of restoration.
