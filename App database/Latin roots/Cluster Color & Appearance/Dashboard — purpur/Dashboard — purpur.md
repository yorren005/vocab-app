## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 How Derived Words are Formed
> The root **purpur** operates through both vernacular sound-shifted and classical Latinate stems:
> - **Vernacular Dissimilated Stem `purpl-`:** Latin *purpura* → Old English *purpul* → English [[purple]] (noun, adjective, and verb).
>   - Causative verbal prefixation: *em- + purple* → [[empurple]] ("to make or become purple").
> - **Direct Latinate Noun Stem `purpur-`:**
>   - Retained Latin nominative in medicine → [[purpura]] (hemorrhagic skin lesions).
>   - Adjectival medical suffix: *purpura + -ic* → [[purpuric]] (*purpuric rash*).
>   - Chemical dye suffixation: *purpura + -in* → [[purpurin]] (trihydroxyanthraquinone pigment).
> - **Heraldic & Poetic Suffixation:**
>   - Anglo-Norman heraldic tincture → [[purpure]].
>   - Poetic adjective: *purpureus + -al* → [[purpureal]].
> - **Canonical Literary & Historical Phrases:**
>   - Horatian rhetoric: [[purpureus pannus]] ("purple patch").
>   - Retinal physiology: [[visual purple]] (rhodopsin).
>   - Byzantine dynastic idiom: *born in the purple* (*porphyrogenitus*).
