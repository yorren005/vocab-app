## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 How Derived Words are Formed
> The root **virid** operates in English through two distinct morphological tracks:
> - **Vernacular French Sound-Shifted Stem `verd-` (Latin *viridis* → Old French *vert / verd*):**
>   - Adjectival participle: *viridīre* → *verdant* → English [[verdant]], with noun [[verdancy]].
>   - Collective noun suffix: *verd- + -ure* → Middle English [[verdure]].
>   - Compound trade noun: *vert de Grece* ("green of Greece") → Middle English [[verdigris]].
>   - Heraldic blazon: Old French *vert* → English heraldic [[vert]].
> - **Learned Classical Stem `virid-` (direct from Latin *viridis*):**
>   - Chemical pigment suffixation: *virid- + -ian* → English [[viridian]].
>   - Inchoative verbal suffixation: Latin *viridēscēns* → English [[viridescent]], [[viridescence]].
>   - Latin theological abstract noun: *virid- + -itās* → English [[viriditas]].
>   - Architectural architectural noun: Latin *viridārium* → English [[viridarium]].
>   - Bacteriological participial epithet: Latin *viridāns* → [[viridans]] (*Streptococcus viridans*).
