## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 How Derived Words are Formed
> The root operates through two twin morphological trunks:
> - **Adjectival Stem:** `suav-` (from *suāvis*, giving *suave*, *suavity*, *suaveness*, *suavely*)
> - **Verbal Present Stem:** `suad-` (from *suādēre*, giving *persuade*, *dissuade*)
> - **Verbal Supine / Participial Stem:** `suas-` (from *suāsum*, giving *suasion*, *persuasion*, *persuasive*, *dissuasion*, *dissuasive*)
> - **French Phonetic Softening:** `assuag-` (from *ad-* + *suāvis*, giving *assuage*, *assuagement*)
>
> By attaching directional and intensive prefixes (`per-` = thoroughly; `dis-` = away, against; `ad-` = toward), Latin engineered the foundational vocabulary of persuasion, deterrence, and emotional consolation.

> [!tip] 🌈 Shades of Meaning in Different Words
> The semantic branches of `suav` divide into four distinct operational arenas:
> - **Social Grace & Sophistication:** *suave*, *suavity*, and *suavely* denote polished, urban, sophisticated, and charming manners (though sometimes with a hint of superficial calculation).
> - **Emotional & Physical Relief:** *assuage* and *assuagement* denote the mitigation, easing, or satisfying of hunger, thirst, pain, grief, or guilt.
> - **Rhetorical Power & Conviction:** *persuade*, *persuasion*, and *persuasive* describe winning over the intellect or will through compelling argument and rhetorical grace.
> - **Deterrence & Cautionary Counsel:** *dissuade*, *dissuasion*, and *dissuasive* denote turning someone away from an unwise or dangerous plan.
