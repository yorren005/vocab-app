## 💡 Core Meaning

> [!example] ✨ Core Concept: law or right
> The root **juris** means law or right. It refers to established rules, legal justice, and social regulations. In English, this root forms words such as *jurisdiction*, *jurisdictional*, *jurisdictionally*, and *jurisprudence*.

### 📌 Key Learning Concepts

- **What It Literally Means**:
  - The root **juris** comes from a Latin word that means *"law or right"*.
  - At its core, it describes law or right.

- **The Big Picture Idea**:
  - Picture a community gathering in a hall to establish fair rules and resolve disputes.
  - Whenever you see **juris** in an English word, think of **justice, legal authority, and governance**.

- **How the Meaning Grows**:
  - **Physical**: The tangible physical presence or real-world form of law or right.
  - **Mental & Social**: How people experience, organize, or communicate about law or right.
  - **Abstract & Practical**: Broader social systems, classifications, or specialized terms.

- **Everyday English Words to Remember It By**:
  - **Jurisdiction**: The official power or authority of a court or judge to hear and determine legal cases and administer justice.
  - **Jurisdictional**: Relating to or bounded by judicial, administrative, or geographical jurisdiction.
  - **Jurisdictionally**: In a jurisdictional manner.
  - **Jurisprudence**: The philosophy, theory, or science of law.

## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

> [!abstract] 🔨 How Derived Words are Formed
> The root operates through four primary morphological channels in English:
> - **Genitive Combining Compound Stem (`juris-`):** Derived from Latin *iūris*: *jurisdiction*, *jurisprudence*, *jurisconsult*, *jurisdictional*.
> - **Direct Latin Legal Nominative (`jus-` / `ius-`):** Preserved in constitutional and international doctrines: *jus cogens*, *jus gentium*, *jus civile*, *jus soli*, *jus sanguinis*, *sui juris*, *de jure*.
> - **Scholarly & Agentive Stem (`jurist-`):** Derived via Medieval Latin *iūrista*: *jurist*, *juristic*, *juristical*.
> - **Privative Injury Stem (`injuri-`):** Formed from Latin *iniūria* (< *in-* "not" + *iūs*): *injury*, *injurious*, *injuriously*, *uninjured*.

> [!tip] 🌈 Shades of Meaning in Different Words
> The derivations of *juris* organize into five primary domains:
> - **Constitutional & Court Jurisdiction:** In [[jurisdiction]], [[jurisdictional]], and [[jurisdictionally]], the root denotes the geographic, territorial, and subject-matter bounds of judicial power.
> - **Legal Philosophy & Legal Science:** In [[jurisprudence]], [[jurisprudential]], [[jurist]], [[juristic]], and [[jurisconsult]], the root represents theoretical scholarship, analytical legal science, and the philosophy of law.
> - **Public International Law & Sovereignty:** In [[jus cogens]], [[jus gentium]], [[jus ad bellum]], and [[jus in bello]], the root establishes peremptory global norms, humanitarian law, and the rules of warfare.
> - **Citizenship, Standing & Legal Capacity:** In [[jus soli]], [[jus sanguinis]], [[sui juris]], and [[alieni juris]], the root defines nationality by birth and the legal competence of an adult individual to enter contracts.
> - **Torts, Bodily Harm & Civil Wrongs:** In [[injury]], [[injurious]], and [[uninjured]], the root preserves the private-law doctrine that physical or reputational harm is fundamentally an act contrary to law (*iniūria*).
