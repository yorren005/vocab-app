### 📌 Key Learning Concepts

  - The root **cogit** comes from a Latin word that means *"to think or ponder"*.

  - Picture sitting quietly while turning ideas over in your mind to solve a problem.
  - Whenever you see **cogit** in an English word, think of **to think or ponder**.

  - **Physical**: Moving, interacting, or handling things physically (to think or ponder).
  - **Mental & Social**: Applying this action to thoughts, speech, feelings, or decisions.
  - **Abstract & Practical**: Formal rules, legal terms, or scientific processes carrying out this function.

- **Everyday English Words to Remember It By**:
  - **Cogitate**: To think deeply about something.
  - **Cogitation**: The action of thinking deeply about something.
  - **Excogitate**: To think out, devise, or invent something by intense mental effort.
  - **Cogitative**: Possessing or exercising the power of thinking or meditating.

## ⚙️ 2. Root Characteristics & Word Formation Engine

The root **cogit** attaches to Latin prefixes and nominal suffixes:
- **Base Verb & Noun Stems (`cogit-` < *cōgitāre*)**:
  - *cōgitāre* $\to$ **cogitate**, **cogitation**, **cogitative**, **cogitator**.
- **Prefix Compounds**:
  - *ex-* ("out, thoroughly") + *cōgitāre* $\to$ **excogitate**, **excogitation**, **excogitative**.
  - *in-* ("not") + *cogitable* $\to$ **incogitable** ("unthinkable").
